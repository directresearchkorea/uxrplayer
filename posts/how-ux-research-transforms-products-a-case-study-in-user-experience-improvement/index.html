How UX Research Transforms Products: A Case Study in User Experience Improvement

Feedback is not collected—it is designed.

And well-designed experiences ultimately change products.

In collaboration with a global “G” company,

we conducted a user experience (UX) improvement project for a content recommendation service tailored to Korean users.

In many global UX improvement processes,

research is often seen as simply collecting opinions.

However, this project was different.

The goal was clear:

not just to gather feedback, but to connect it to actual product improvements.

To achieve this, we combined qualitative and quantitative research,designing and operating a structured participation experienceso that users could fully engage with the serviceand provide meaningful, experience-based feedback.

1. Long-term UX Research Design: Improving the Quality of Insights

Content feeds are difficult to fully understand through one-time surveys or interviews alone.

This project was a 7-month longitudinal UX research study,

where building a sustainable participation environment was key.

Providing clear guidance on how to explore the service

Designing an operational structure for long-term participation

Enabling users to naturally accumulate experiences in their daily content consumption

Through continuous communication and operational support,

we maintained stable participation throughout the project.

As a result,

users provided experience-based UX feedback grounded in their daily usage,

allowing us to capture data enriched with context and time-based insights.

2. AI Feature Rollout and Stabilization: From Experience to Actionable UX Insights

During the project, an AI feature was introduced and stabilized within the service.

Rather than simply collecting opinions, we designed the participation structure so that the feature could be naturally experienced within real user flows and lead to meaningful feedback.

Providing clear guidance on the feature’s purpose and usage context

Encouraging natural interaction within existing user journeys

Enabling repeated use to generate substantive feedback

Within the long-term participation structure, users were able to experience the feature seamlessly as part of their everyday content consumption.

As a result, concrete, actionable UX insights were generated,

which led to AI feature strategies better aligned with local users.

These insights were reflected in actual product improvements,

enhancing personalized content deliveryand leading to more positive user responses.

UX research does not end with data—it drives real change.

3. Global Collaboration: Connecting Local UX Insights to Decision-Making

This project was carried out in collaboration with the global “G” team.

We went beyond simple data delivery,

playing a key role in connecting local user experience insights with global decision-making.

Managing participation flow through direct communication with users

Rapidly organizing and sharing UX feedback

Quickly capturing user reactions to feature changes

Flexibly responding to global team requests

Through this continuous feedback loop,

we ensured that research and product improvement remained tightly connected.

As a result, we achieved high participant engagement and stable data quality,

received positive feedback from the global team,

and enabled insights to be quickly reflected in actual decision-making.

Conclusion: UX Research Is Completed Through Experience

UX research is not simply about asking questions and collecting answers.

It is about designing environments where users can fully experience a product,

and connecting those experiences to real product improvements.

Ultimately, what matters is not the volume of data,

but the kind of user experience from which the insights are derived.
