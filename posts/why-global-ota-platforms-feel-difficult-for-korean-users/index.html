Why Global OTA Platforms Feel Difficult for Korean Users

Published on 2026-06-26

Why does "Global Standard" design cause high cognitive fatigue for Korean users? In this article, we reveal the local friction points of Korean travelers, uncovered through online community exploration, in-situ interviews, and an AI-assisted research workflow.

Recently, our team successfully completed a project analyzing the digital user journey of Korean travelers for a leading global Online Travel Agency (OTA) platform. This research was designed to capture real-world behavioral patterns and the contextual nuances of how Korean users plan trips and search for accommodations in their daily lives. To achieve this, we designed a hybrid research approach combining online community exploration (bulletin board analysis) with in-situ interviews to closely observe users in their actual environments.

Research Innovation: Rigorous Validation and AI Workflows

In global qualitative research, the ultimate goals are data reliability and speed. Our team led the entire process—from participant recruitment and meticulous moderation to close collaboration with global partners—to extract high-density qualitative insights.

For this project, we integrated AI translation technology into our workflow to efficiently process a massive volume of interview scripts. This drastically reduced the barrier and cost of script translation, which is traditionally a major bottleneck in global UX research.

However, we did not rely on technology alone. Our professional researchers cross-checked and refined the raw AI-translated data, injecting local context, cultural nuances, and domain-specific knowledge of the Korean travel market. By preserving the depth and reliability of qualitative data while accelerating the translation process, we delivered the comprehensive summary report ahead of schedule, exceeding our client's expectations.

Listening to the User: Local Pain Points of Global OTAs

By cross-referencing clues from online communities with behavioral observations from our in-situ interviews, we identified critical friction points that global platforms must address to drive true user lock-in in the Korean market:

1. Information Overload and "Cognitive Fatigue"

Korean users are accustomed to highly intuitive, clean, and structured UIs. In contrast, the layouts of global platforms, which often present dense blocks of information without clear hierarchy, quickly exhaust users during their search journey. Rather than crowding the screen with data, global platforms need to modernize their UI/UX to match the scanning and navigation speed typical of Korean users.

2. The Safety Net: Trust and Psychological Comfort

Korean travelers seek to minimize uncertainty, particularly regarding the gap between polished promotional photos and the actual condition of accommodations. They prioritize psychological safety throughout their entire journey, from discovery and payment to post-booking support. To build lasting trust, platforms must integrate local easy-payment systems (such as Kakao Pay, Naver Pay, etc.) to streamline checkout and establish localized customer care infrastructure that guarantees smooth communication during unexpected situations.

Beyond "Global Standards" to the "Translation of Experience"

This project highlighted that localization is about more than just translating text; it requires the "translation of experience"—fully understanding the cultural expectations, emotions, and behavioral habits of the target market. Korean travelers are exceptionally tech-savvy, fast-paced, and accustomed to highly sophisticated digital convenience.

Combining a multi-dimensional approach (integrating online and offline insights) with a smart, technology-driven research workflow allowed us to provide clear direction for a global brand entering the Korean market.
