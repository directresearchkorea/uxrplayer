G-STAR 2025 operated on a smaller scale compared to previous years, but it delivered one of the clearest and most actionable views of where Korea's game industry is headed next. The event revealed four structural shifts that are now shaping the market:

## 1. A Clear Return to PC and Console as Core Platforms

Despite reduced exhibition space, behavioral patterns showed a strong move back toward high-fidelity experiences. Playable builds for AION 2, Solo Leveling: KARMA, and Palworld Mobile generated the longest queues, reflecting a shift toward precision control and tactile gameplay depth.

## 2. Accelerated Expansion of K-Game IP Across Media Ecosystems

K-game IPs are evolving into global brands, extending into webtoons, animation, and soundtracks. Consistency in worldbuilding and brand identity across platforms is becoming crucial for UX and community-building.

## 3. UX-Centered Playable Demos as the New Industry Standard

The event functioned as a large-scale usability lab. Demos were optimized for first-minute UX, with core mechanics surfaced quickly (3–5 mins) and sessions structured into compact loops (8–10 mins).

## 4. Narrative-Driven Development Gains Strategic Importance

Story-first design is becoming a major competitive layer. Players respond to coherence and narrative persistence, which are key for long-term engagement.

## Structural Weaknesses

Reduced diversity and the removal of the subculture zone diminished the variety of observable user behaviors.
